# Select a circular storm drain

Delineate each catchment and identify the governing duration and return period. Where the rational method applies, peak L/s = 2.778 × runoff coefficient × intensity (mm/h) × catchment (ha). Use the local intensity-duration-frequency curve at the time of concentration. At confluences reassess the contributing areas and common design duration. CPHEEO Storm Water Drainage Systems (2019), Part A §§4.4.1 and 5.2–5.3.6.

For a circular bore D metres and fill fraction y = depth/D, θ = 2 acos(1 − 2y) in radians, wetted area A = D²(θ − sin θ)/8, wetted perimeter P = Dθ/2. Manning velocity v = (A/P)^(2/3) × √S / n; capacity = 1000Av in L/s. Use positive hydraulic slope S for uniform free-surface flow. For operating velocity, solve for y at the actual flow on the increasing-flow branch up to the allowed fill.

Example SW1: 0.40 ha, C=0.70 and local design intensity 75 mm/h give 58.3 L/s. The **example project** permits 0.75D at design flow. At n=0.013 and slope 0.005, calculated capacities at that fill are 38.34, 62.35 and 94.05 L/s for 250, 300 and 350 mm **internal** bores. At another n or slope multiply by (0.013/n) × √(S/0.005); at another permitted fill recompute the section.

250 mm fails. The 300 mm candidate carries the duty at about 0.71D and 1.09 m/s under free discharge. Check inlet capture, access, minimum bore, sediment, tailwater and surcharge. If the outfall level is missing, retain the provisional candidate and flag the downstream water level check. Use the actual commercial bore, material and load class to match a pipe item. Report reinstatement and excavation ownership once per crossing.
