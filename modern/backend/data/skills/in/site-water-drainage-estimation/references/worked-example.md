# Site water and drainage estimation — worked example

Storm profile D2 rev B. Nodes A, B and C have invert levels 101.00, 100.80 and 100.50 m. Horizontal runs AB = 40 m, BC = 60 m; both slopes = 0.005. The example schedule pays centre-to-centre length: book 40 + 60 = 100 m, with three chambers including B once. At a constant authorised trench width 0.8 m and depth to formation 1.5 m, gross trench excavation is 120 m³. Adjust chamber overlap to the item convention before adding chamber excavation.

For an example 0.40 ha catchment, C = 0.70 and supplied intensity 75 mm/h give 58.3 L/s. A trial 350 mm inside diameter flowing full at n = 0.013 and slope 0.005 gives about 0.103 m³/s. This full-flow screening value needs the authority's permitted depth check. Missing tailwater prevents confirmation of free discharge; keep the design provisional and identify the source of the measured pipe size.

## Pressure water and foul flow

For a separate fixture, the employer supplies 200 users, 100 L/person/day and a peak factor of 3. Average demand is 20,000 L/day and peak flow is 0.694 L/s. A candidate with 40 mm internal diameter gives about 0.55 m/s. Record that candidate against its reach. Without the connection pressure, elevation change and line losses, residual pressure and the final diameter remain open.

For the foul network, an example brief returns 80% of that daily demand to sewer. With a separately specified foul peak factor of 3 and 0.10 L/s infiltration, the design flow is (16,000 / 86,400) × 3 + 0.10 = 0.656 L/s. Use the pipe's part-full depth and velocity for its gradient. Neither example supplies a municipal demand or infiltration default.
