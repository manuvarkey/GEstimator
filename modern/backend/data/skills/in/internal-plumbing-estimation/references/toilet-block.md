# Fixture load and preliminary sizes

Under SP 7:2026 Part E Section 1 Tables 2–3, an ordinary public flush-tank WC contributes 3 water supply fixture units (WSFU), a standard-faucet basin 1.5, and a metered-faucet basin 1. Each of these fixture supply branches has a 15 mm nominal minimum. Enhanced public usage, flush valves, hot water and continuous flow change the design basis. Check actual draw-off requirements, especially for small groups and synchronised use.

For four cistern WCs and four standard basins, cold only, the shared supply serves 18 WSFU. The flush-tank column gives 30.3 L/min at 10 WSFU and 53.0 L/min at 20. For a conservative shortlist use the next row, 53.0 L/min = 0.883 L/s. Check the actual pipe bore against the available head and demand. The 0.60 L/s pressure example in the guide has its own supplied duty.

Use separate drainage fixture units (DFU). Under Part E Section 2 Table 20 an ordinary public cistern WC contributes 4 DFU, minimum trap 80 mm, and a basin 1 DFU, minimum trap 32 mm subject to outlet compatibility. Two of each per floor give 10 DFU per branch interval and 20 DFU total over two floors. Check additional floor drains separately.

For a proposed 100 mm stack and horizontal common branch, Table 22 limits at that diameter are 160 DFU on the horizontal fixture branch, 90 entering one stack interval and 240 on a stack of up to three intervals. The illustrated loads fit those limits. For the separate 100 mm building drain at 1:100, Table 23 gives 180 DFU. Confirm fixture connections, gradient and actual invert, cleanouts, stack offsets, ventilation under Table 19 and any hydraulic or local minima before issuing sizes. The building sewer uses its own capacity table.
