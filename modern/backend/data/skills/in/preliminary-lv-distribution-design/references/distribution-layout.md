# Choose the distribution arrangement

Start the single-line schedule with building height in metres, occupancy, floor and room uses, supply/earthing system, source panel, load and route. Apply the following office preferences subject to statutory fire performance, authority conditions, contract and verified equipment limits.

## Indoor circuits and isolation

Prefer insulated single copper conductors in conduit: PVC conduit for recessed runs, steel for surface or suspended runs. Check fire classification, mechanical exposure, corrosion, bonding of metallic containment, support and accessible draw points. Select insulation and sheath to the fire duty. CEA 38(3)(iv) specifies FRLSH power cables above 15 m and HFFR power cables for airports, hospitals and hotels irrespective of height. Use compatible cable ratings for the actual construction. Map conduit in masonry or insulation to its own IS 732 installation reference method before sizing.

Use 1.5 mm² copper minimum for light wiring and 4 mm² copper minimum for power wiring as office defaults. IS 732:2019, 5.2.7.1 Table 9 gives 1.5 and 2.5 mm² respectively. CPWD Internal 2023, 3.12.1–3.12.3 specifies copper for submain, circuit and point wiring, gives the 1.5/4 minima and calls for calculation above 1 kW. Increase size for duty, route, grouping, drop and fault protection. Use circuit-appropriate protective devices.

Schedule an accessible isolation arrangement for **each room or independently operated space**. Record room ID, supplying DB, circuit IDs, local isolator or room SDB and which loads stay live. Where the brief requires a room panel, provide an identified SDB/incomer for that room; locate it accessibly under CPWD 3.19 where adopted. A group of switches for lights alone does not isolate socket, HVAC or alternate supplies. Identify essential circuits separately and coordinate emergency isolation.

## Vertical distribution and trays

Prefer a rising main above G+1 (G+2 upwards). Apply CEA 2023 regulation 38(3) independently: its scope includes multi-storeyed buildings over 15 m and the listed premises, including academic buildings, hospitals and hotels. Within scope, (v) requires busbar trunking for distribution to floors. Record occupancy even below 15 m. For example, screen a two-storey school under the academic-building provision; a G+2 residential block under 15 m still has the office riser preference. Verify measured height and local fire/inspector conditions. [Gazette, regulation 38](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2023/246561.pdf)

Show main panel → busbar end feed → protected floor tap-off → floor DB → room isolation. Rate the busbar, neutral, protective path and tap-offs for diversified demand, harmonics, fault duty and coordination. Count riser sections, supports, floor penetrations and fire stops; segregate power shafts under 38(3)(iii). Preserve dedicated safety-service supplies and fire survival where required. CPWD Internal 3.1, 3.19 and 7.3 describe the equipment and access arrangements.

For multiple indoor cable feeders prefer a supported tray with verified loading, grouping, bend radius, segregation, fire performance and accessible terminations. Use individual protected drops or final-circuit conduit as appropriate. Select the actual thermal method for a solid tray, perforated tray, ladder or enclosed section; a tray alone does not establish free-air capacity.

## External runs and street lighting

- Clear open ground with confirmed wayleave, recoverable surface and little prospect of excavation: prefer protected direct burial of a cable rated for burial. Establish depth, spacing, mechanical covers, markers and thermal backfill from the approved detail. Include cushioning for rocky or unknown soil in the estimate. Under CPWD External 2023, 2.6.7–2.6.8, omit cushioning only on stretches meeting the specified exception and expressly identified in the tender. Unverified soil leaves both excavation class and thermal rating provisional.
- Congested services, roads or finished paving: prefer dedicated ducts/raceways and accessible draw chambers at route changes and pulling limits. Coordinate surveyed clearances, crossing approvals, traffic loading, drainage, spare ways and reinstatement owner. Size chambers by bend radius and pulling geometry. Measure route metres once, duct metres per way, and cable metres per installed run.
- Prefer separate single-phase streetlighting radials where loading and voltage drop permit. Use a three-core cable with identified L, N and PE. Balance independent radials across supply phases at the pillar, each with its own neutral. For a necessary three-phase, four-wire feeder, schedule L1/L2/L3/N plus a continuous copper PE alongside it; designate insulated PE or suitably protected bare copper as permitted by the earthing design. Check PE size, corrosion, bonds to pillars/poles and fault disconnection. Keep PE separate from neutral.
- For a shared-neutral feeder, assess phase imbalance and LED harmonics, the neutral cross-section and termination integrity. A lost neutral can expose phase-to-neutral loads to damaging overvoltage. Coordinate linked isolation/switching where required by the earthing system; avoid any independent neutral interruption that leaves phases live. Check the farthest pole using segment currents and actual phase loading. A three-phase backbone feeding balanced single-phase loads can be appropriate when long independent radials fail the load or drop check.

For the estimate, assign cable, protection, trench/duct or tray, chambers, rises, terminations and reinstatement to their measured item or composite inclusion. Hand route IDs to the external electrical skill.
